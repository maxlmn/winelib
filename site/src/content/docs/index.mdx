## Every appellation, on the map

Every appellation of France, the EU, the US and Australia, outlined from the official registers, with Burgundy's
premiers crus. Regions in their colours, and your cellar and tastings on them. A wine's page shows its appellation
and its vineyard; a region's page shows where what you drink comes from. IGN's aerial photos in France.
</div>
<Shot name="worldmap" alt="The world map, wine regions in their colours" />
</div>

<div class="feature flip">
<div>
## Down to the lieu-dit

Les Poyeux, Clos des Noëls, Les Gravières: the names on the labels are the lieux-dits of France's land register.
WineLib draws 91,000 of them across the Loire, Burgundy, Champagne, Beaujolais, Jura, Alsace, Bordeaux and the
Rhône, each with the appellations it can claim. Click one to make it your wine's vineyard.
</div>

## Your cellar, rack by rack

Each place you keep bottles, drawn as a wine rack: a bottle end per bottle in its region's colour, the wine's colour
as the cork, magnums bigger. Then a shelf map bin by bin, a vintage chart, and what your bottles are worth.
</div>
<Shot name="cellar" alt="The cellar as wine racks, bottle ends in their region's colours" />

## Lists, a treasure hunt

A critic's top 100, Noble Rot's wines of the decade, the 1855 classification, your wishlist. Each entry ticks
itself once you've tasted it, and each list is a mosaic of bottle ends that fills in as you go.
</div>

## A tasting journal

Tastings by occasion (a dinner, a restaurant, a trip), with your notes, ratings and photos, and the places on a
map. Share any tasting, or a whole evening's wines, as a sticker for your Instagram story.
</div>

## The grapes' family

635 grapes with their colour, origin and parents (from VIVC), a description of each, and where it's planted. A
network of every known parent and offspring, with your wines on it.
</div>

## On your phone

The same app in a phone layout, installable with its own icon. Reach it from anywhere through your own private
network (Tailscale, for instance).

## And more

- **A wine list to print**: your cellar or a selection, laid out like a restaurant's, saved as a PDF.
- **Ask your AI assistant**: an [MCP server](mcp/) lets Claude read your cellar and journal (*what should I open
  from home tonight?*).
- **A bottle from photos of its labels**, read by Claude with your own key.
- **Imports** from CellarTracker, Vivino or any spreadsheet; **export** of everything as JSON or CSV.
- **Critics' scores** from any critic, **market prices** by LWIN, **style tags**, **blends**, **vintage pages**.
- **Light or dark**, in nine colour palettes.

## Free, and yours

WineLib is open source (MIT). It runs on your computer or your home server, with Docker or Python: no account, no
cloud, no subscription. [Install it](install/) in a few minutes.
