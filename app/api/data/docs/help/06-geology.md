# Reading the geology

What a vineyard's Geology card shows: the rock under it, the colours, the notations, and the French words.

## What it is

The formations under the vineyard's outline, each with its share of it (a bar, then a line each, largest first). In
France they come from BRGM's geological maps at 1:50,000, in the map pack: the rock as mapped at the surface, or the
deposits covering it (slope wash, scree, river gravels). It's the geology under the soil, not the soil itself. Elsewhere
the card shows Macrostrat's map at the vineyard, often regional (about 1:1,000,000 in Europe): a broad idea, not the
detail of one cru.

The descriptions are BRGM's own, in French, as published.

A rule of thumb on the card: pale greys, creams and beiges are recent deposits over the rock (slope wash, scree, river
gravels); greens are chalk and tuffeau; blues the Upper Jurassic marls and limestones; browns and ochres the Côte's
Middle Jurassic limestones; pinks and reds the old crystalline rock.

## The notations

The short code before each description is the map's: a letter for the period (the timeline's *Code*), a number for
the stage, older to younger, sometimes a letter for a part of it.

- **j**: j1 Aalenian, j2 Bajocian, j3 Bathonian, j4 Callovian, j5 Oxfordian, then the Kimmeridgian and Tithonian
- **c**: c1 Cenomanian, c2 Turonian, c3 Coniacian, then the Santonian to Maastrichtian (the Senonian)
- **F** (Fv, Fw, Fx, Fy, Fz): river alluvium, from the highest, oldest terraces to the valley floor · **C**: colluvium
  (slope wash) · **E**: scree · **OE**: loess
- Crystalline rocks (granites, gneiss) have codes in Greek letters, left out of the card.

So **j3a** is part of the Bathonian, **c2c** the upper Turonian, **Fy** a recent river terrace.
